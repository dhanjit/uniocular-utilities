# Prescription safety eyewear: lens and frame failure (Vinger & Woods, 2000)

**Citation:** Vinger PF, Woods TA. "Prescription safety eyewear: impact
studies of lens and frame failure." *Optometry*. 2000;71(2):91–103.
**Identifiers:** PMID 10970253.
**Access:** Full report free from the ASSP Foundation
(<https://foundation.assp.org/docs/study_vinger1998.pdf>).
**Verification:** Verified — figures and quotations below checked against
the ASSP full-text report and the PubMed abstract (2026-11-12).

## Scope

Laboratory study of 641 plano and prescription (−3.00 and +3.00 D)
polycarbonate lenses of varying thickness and edge geometry on steel lens
holders, and 128 prescription safety frames on headforms, impacted with
ANSI Z87 test objects and sports balls, filmed at 500–2,000 frames per second.

## Key points

- Describes ANSI Z87.1-1989: high-velocity test with a **6.35 mm (1/4-inch)
  steel ball at 45.7 m/s (150 ft/s)**, no eye contact allowed; high-mass test
  with a pointed **500 g** mass dropped **130 cm (51.2 in)**. Rx lenses then
  had to pass only a 1-inch ball drop and were "permitted to fail with the
  high-mass and high-velocity tests". Minimum thickness: plano passing high
  velocity 2.0 mm, plano failing it 3.0 mm, over +3.00 in the most-plus
  meridian 2.5 mm, all other Rx 3 mm.
- Under the 1/4-inch ball, **every lens that shattered had a CT of 1.8 mm or
  less** (one plano 1.8 mm at 150 ft/s; −3.00 lenses of 1.1 and 1.5 mm at
  250 ft/s).
- Under the 500 g mass, perforation was related to CT; lenses **below 2.2 mm**
  perforated significantly more often (38 of 87 perforated overall).
- "Thinner lenses were more flexible and prone to 'oil can' out of the frame
  than 3mm minimal thickness lenses." "3mm minimum thickness lenses may be
  necessary for high-impact Rx eyewear, but thinner lenses may be adequate if
  the frame is designed to retain the lens".
- Plano lenses were at least as prone to failure as −3.00 or +3.00 lenses of
  the same minimum thickness.
- **More than 40% of safety frames with removable lenses broke or lost
  lenses** at energies expected in industry and sport. The best retention
  used a frame bevel perpendicular to a frontal impact.
- A well-designed motorcycle goggle retained a 0.78 mm polycarbonate lens
  against a golf ball at 60 mph; spectacles at 13–15 mm vertex distance have
  less room to absorb energy, so need thicker lenses.

## Learnings for this project

- The best primary evidence for a **minimum center thickness** (about 2 mm,
  3 mm for high-impact use) even in polycarbonate.
- Frame and lens retention matter as much as the lens; a strong lens in a
  weak frame can still reach the eye.
