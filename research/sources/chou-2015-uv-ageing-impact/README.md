# Ultraviolet exposure and impact resistance of ophthalmic lenses (Chou, Dain & Cheng, 2015)

**Citation:** Chou BR, Dain SJ, Cheng BB. "Effect of Ultraviolet Exposure on
Impact Resistance of Ophthalmic Lenses." *Optometry and Vision Science*.
2015;92(12):1154–1160.
**Identifiers:** PMID 26540475; DOI
[10.1097/OPX.0000000000000730](https://doi.org/10.1097/OPX.0000000000000730).
**Access:** Paywalled; abstract on PubMed.
**Verification:** Verified — figures below checked against the PubMed
abstract (2026-11-12).

## Scope

Plano CR-39, Hoya Phoenix, Younger Trilogy and polycarbonate lenses, all
nominally **2 mm** thick, with various SR and AR coatings, in batches of 40.
Half of each batch was UV-conditioned to EN 168. All were struck with 6 mm
steel balls; mean breakage velocity by ZEST protocol.

## Key points

- **Unexposed SR-coated polycarbonate could not be broken** with the test
  apparatus; after UV exposure its mean breakage velocity was **142 m/s**.
- UV exposure lowered breakage velocity in every substrate: uncoated CR-39
  −10.3 m/s; CR-39 with ultra-hard coat −3.5; CR-39 with AR and SR −4.1;
  Phoenix SR −4.8, AR −3.7; Trilogy SR −3.9, **AR −17.8 m/s** (all p < 0.05).
- "Protective lenses that have been exposed to high levels of UVR for
  extended periods should be replaced regularly ... even if they do not show
  visible damage".

## Learnings for this project

- The newest independent lab comparison found (2015), and the only one that
  ran polycarbonate and Trivex-type lenses in the same rig at the same 2 mm
  thickness: polycarbonate did not break; the others did.
- Supports periodic replacement of everyday protective lenses.
