## Scope

US clinical guideline for managing refractive error in adults and children:
spectacles, contact lenses, refractive surgery, myopia control. Eye
protection appears under the indications for spectacles.

## Key points

- p. P15: "Shatterproof eyeglasses are also recommended for all individuals
  with good vision in only one eye. When ocular protection is the foremost
  consideration, polycarbonate plastic is the material of choice because it is
  much more impact resistant than regular plastic or hardened glass." The
  evidence cited is [vinger-1997-lens-impact](../vinger-1997-lens-impact/).
  "Depending on the activity, frames with side protection may be important."
- p. P15, separately: "Safety glasses or eye protectors are strongly
  recommended for individuals involved in certain sports (e.g., racquetball,
  squash) and hazardous activities in which there is risk of flying
  particles".
- p. P16: "The risks of complications associated with contact lenses should
  be weighed against the protective benefit of eyeglasses for monocular or
  functionally monocular patients."
- p. P37: "Eyeglasses are protective, however, which is especially important
  for monocular patients."
- Names **no** other material (no Trivex) and sets **no** thickness. The
  one-eye sentence carries no time qualifier ("always" or "for activities").

## Learnings for this project

- The highest-level adult guideline: impact-resistant ("shatterproof")
  spectacles for anyone with one good eye, with polycarbonate as the named
  material. Activity-specific advice is extra.
- The guideline's silence on Trivex and thickness is why the project leans on
  lab studies and other guidance for those details.
