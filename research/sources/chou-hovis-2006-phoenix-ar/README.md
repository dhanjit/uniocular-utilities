# Antireflection coatings and impact resistance of Hoya Phoenix lenses (Chou & Hovis, 2006)

**Citation:** Chou BR, Hovis JK. "Effect of multiple antireflection coatings
on impact resistance of Hoya Phoenix spectacle lenses." *Clinical and
Experimental Optometry*. 2006;89(2):86–89.
**Identifiers:** PMID 16494611; DOI
[10.1111/j.1444-0938.2006.00013.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1444-0938.2006.00013.x).
**Access:** Paywalled; abstract on PubMed.
**Verification:** Verified — figures below checked against the PubMed
abstract (2026-11-12).

## Scope

Four groups of plano Hoya Phoenix (Trivex-type urethane) lenses: dress and
industrial thickness, each with scratch-resistant (SR) coat alone or SR plus
multilayer AR (MAR). Edged to 50 mm, mounted in a lens support, struck at the
center by a 6.35 mm steel ball; threshold breakage speed by ZEST protocol.

## Key points

- Threshold breakage speed, SR only: **55.1 m/s** (dress), **63.2 m/s**
  (industrial).
- With SR + MAR: **50.1 m/s** (dress), **54.7 m/s** (industrial) — about 9%
  and 13% lower; all comparisons p < 0.005.
- Mean impact resistance exceeded the eye-protector requirements of AS/NZS
  1337:1992, **ANSI Z87.1-2003** and CSA Z94.3-02.
- "Unlike polycarbonate lenses, dress thickness Phoenix lenses do not display
  'oilcanning' deformation on high energy impact and therefore are less
  likely to be dislodged from their mountings."

## Learnings for this project

- Independent evidence that Trivex-type lenses meet occupational high-velocity
  requirements even at dress thickness.
- AR coating costs a measurable but modest share of impact resistance in this
  material.
