# AR coatings, thickness and polycarbonate penetration by pointed missiles (Chou, Gupta & Hovis, 2005)

**Citation:** Chou BR, Gupta A, Hovis JK. "The effect of multiple
antireflective coatings and center thickness on resistance of polycarbonate
spectacle lenses to penetration by pointed missiles." *Optometry and Vision
Science*. 2005;82(11):964–969.
**Identifiers:** PMID 16317372; DOI
[10.1097/01.opx.0000187846.82423.94](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.opx.0000187846.82423.94).
**Access:** Paywalled; abstract on PubMed.
**Verification:** Verified — figures below checked against the PubMed
abstract (2026-11-12).

## Scope

Surfaced plano polycarbonate lenses, 2 mm or 3 mm center thickness, with an
SR coat alone or SR plus multilayer AR. Struck by an industrial sewing-machine
needle in an aluminium carrier.

## Key points

- The needle pierced the lenses at **29.6–46.2 m/s**.
- Resistance was lowest for **thinner** lenses and **AR-coated** lenses; the
  two effects were subadditive.
- Polycarbonate is more vulnerable to sharp, fast missiles than blunt ones.
- "the use of 2-mm center thickness and MAR-coated polycarbonate lenses
  should be discouraged for industrial eye protectors where sharp missile
  hazards are possible."

## Learnings for this project

- Polycarbonate is not invulnerable; thickness and coating choices still
  matter, especially against sharp debris.
