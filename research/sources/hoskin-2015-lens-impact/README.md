# Spectacle-related eye injuries, impact performance and eye protection (Hoskin et al., 2015)

**Citation:** Hoskin AK, Philip S, Dain SJ, Mackey DA. "Spectacle-related eye
injuries, spectacle-impact performance and eye protection." *Clinical and
Experimental Optometry*. 2015;98(3):203–209.
**Identifiers:** PMID 25963111; DOI
[10.1111/cxo.12283](https://doi.org/10.1111/cxo.12283).
**Access:** Paywalled (publisher returned 403); a copy is hosted in the AOA
evidence library (<https://www.aoa.org/assets/documents/ebo/adult%20eye/415.07hoskin.pdf>),
which was unavailable (HTTP 503) on 2026-11-12.
**Verification:** Citation and abstract verified via PubMed (2026-11-12). The
review's tables (penetration thresholds by material, projectile energies,
standards energies) were **not** re-verified; figures from them below are
**from search digest** (an earlier set of notes) and must not be promoted
without checking the tables.

## Scope

Literature review of spectacle-related ocular trauma (695 individual injuries
identified), spectacle lens impact performance, and impact requirements of
international, regional and national standards. Excludes work before 1975.

## Key points

Verified (abstract):

- Spectacle-related injuries mostly occurred in sport (balls, racquets, bats,
  collisions).
- Frame, lens design and material choice were associated with injury;
  **polycarbonate is cited as the material of choice** in the literature.
- Standards vary widely in their impact requirements for prescription lenses,
  sports and occupational protection.
- At particular risk: people prone to falls, **the functionally one-eyed**,
  people with corneal thinning, and those with previous eye surgery or injury.

From search digest (not re-verified; tables):

- BB / pellet penetration thresholds compiled from earlier studies: glass
  84 m/s (2.4 J); CR-39 97.7 m/s (3.4 J); high index 107.7 m/s (4.1 J);
  polycarbonate spectacles 210 m/s (58.8 J). The glass and high-index figures
  match [rychwalski-2003-airgun-impact](../rychwalski-2003-airgun-impact/),
  which *is* verified.
- Standards energies: FDA / ANSI Z80 drop-ball 0.20 J; ANSI Z87.1 basic impact
  0.85 J; EN 166 "F" 0.87 J; ASTM F803 sports 18.64 J.
- Real-world projectile energies: a stone meeting a vehicle at highway speed
  about 69 J; a baseball about 125 J.
- Reported text: reduced center thickness and coatings such as AR "have been
  shown to reduce lens impact resistance"; rimless or metal frames and small
  vertex distance appear in injury case reports.

## Learnings for this project

- A good single entry point to the lens-impact literature and the standards
  landscape, and it names the functionally one-eyed as a high-risk group.
- Its compiled table is worth checking against the full text before the
  project quotes any of its numbers.
