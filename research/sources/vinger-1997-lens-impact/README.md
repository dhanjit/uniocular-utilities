# Shatter resistance of spectacle lenses (Vinger et al., 1997)

**Citation:** Vinger PF, Parver L, Alfaro DV, Woods T, Abrams BS. "Shatter
resistance of spectacle lenses." *JAMA*. 1997;277(2):142–144.
**Identifiers:** PMID 8990340.
**Access:** Paywalled; abstract on PubMed
(<https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8990340/>).
**Verification:** Verified — citation, design and conclusions checked against
the PubMed abstract (2026-11-12). The per-lens shatter energies in the full
text were **not** retrieved (JAMA full text unreachable), so no numeric
thresholds from this paper are reproduced here.

## Scope

Laboratory study, 348 impacts. Seven lenses meeting ANSI Z80 dress standards
(high-index plastic, allyl resin plastic [CR-39], heat-tempered glass,
chemically tempered glass, polycarbonate; center thickness 1–2.2 mm) and four
meeting ANSI Z87 industrial standards (allyl resin, two tempered glasses,
polycarbonate; all 3.0 mm). Projectiles: air-gun pellets, golf balls, tennis
balls, lacrosse balls and baseballs. Outcome: impact energy needed to shatter
each lens.

## Key points

- Dress and industrial lenses of glass, allyl resin plastic and **high-index
  plastic shattered at impact energies lower than those expected from the
  projectiles in routine use**.
- **Polycarbonate** resisted all tested projectiles above the impact potential
  expected in routine use.
- The authors concluded that ANSI Z80 and Z87 "should be reevaluated".

## Learnings for this project

- The core evidence behind the AAO's "polycarbonate is the material of choice"
  for people with one good eye (it is reference 174 in the 2022 Refractive
  Errors PPP).
- Meeting a dress-lens standard does not make a brittle material protective.
